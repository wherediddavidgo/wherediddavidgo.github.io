I am a 2nd year PhD Student in the [Global Rivers Group](https://globalriversgroup.com) in the Department of Geosciences at Virginia Tech.

<!-- ![Example river mask](https://wherediddavidgo.github.io/images/rivermask.png "Example river mask") -->

<img src="https://wherediddavidgo.github.io/images/rivermask.png" class="img-right" alt="mask photo">

River networks are hotspots of greenhouse gas exchange, and a key parameter used in estimating greenhouse gas flux is surface area. I use satellite remote sensing and fieldwork to understand how river networks expand and contract in length and width from the headwater to the continental scale to constrain river surface area for better carbon accounting and explore connections between hydrology, geomorphology, and biogeochemistry.

I am also a member of the [Remote Sensing Interdisciplinary Graduate Education Program](https://rsigep.frec.vt.edu/), which allows me to connect with students in such diverse departments as Computer Science, Forestry, and Science, Technology, and Society over our shared interest in remote sensing.

<!-- ![Fieldwork on a salt marsh](https://wherediddavidgo.github.io/images/marshpic.jpeg) -->

Prior to coming to Virginia Tech, I got a BS in Geological Sciences at the University of North Carolina. I spent time working with Professors Emily Eidam, Tony Rodriguez, and Chris Martens at various times as an undergraduate researcher.
